# Examples

## What is this
The practice of writing not about a phenomenon in the abstract, but about your own experience, a real case, or an observed situation.

## Why it's in the strategy
An example makes the text human and distinguishes it from a generic article anyone could find in a search engine.

## When to use
- When abstract advice sounds correct but doesn't land.
- When you need to add credibility and the feeling of real life.

## How to build this type of post
1. Find where you're stating a general principle.
2. Replace it with your own observation, a scene, a result, or a behavioral pattern you witnessed.
3. Keep the example short but dense with detail.
4. Connect the example back to the thesis it's meant to support.

## Examples
- Instead of "To be productive, plan your day properly, set priorities, and take breaks" — "I used to start the day with easy tasks. By evening I had no energy left for the hard ones. Now I do the three hardest things first. Everything else fits around them."
- Instead of "Good sleep is essential for focus and performance" — "Once I started sleeping at least 7 hours, I stopped needing three coffees just to function before noon."
- Instead of "Writing things down helps with self-reflection" — "I write for 10 minutes before bed. Works better than any app I've tried. I also fall asleep faster."

## What to keep in mind
- Readers want your perspective and your experience. Without it, they'd just read a blog post.
