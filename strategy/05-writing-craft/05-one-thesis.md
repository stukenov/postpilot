# One Thesis

## What is this
A rule that limits each post to a single core idea — and cuts everything else.

## Why it's in the strategy
It protects the text from becoming a jumble of half-developed thoughts and makes it easier to read, remember, and share.

## When to use
- Always, when the impulse is to pack an entire topic into one post.
- Especially important for longer posts, where it's easy to drift into a second or third angle.

## How to build this type of post
1. Before writing, state the main thesis in one sentence.
2. Remove everything that doesn't strengthen that thesis.
3. If a second strong idea appears while writing, save it for a separate post.
4. Test: can someone summarize what this post was about in one sentence?

## Examples
- A post about one specific mistake in the first 30 seconds of a sales call — not about sales calls in general.
- A post about one reason client retention drops — not a full breakdown of every retention lever.

## What to keep in mind
- More shorter posts beat one long post mixing multiple ideas. Each idea earns its own moment.
