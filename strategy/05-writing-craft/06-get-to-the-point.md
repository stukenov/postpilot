# Get to the Point

## What is this
Dropping warm-up phrasing like "I've been thinking about this lately" in favor of starting with the idea itself.

## Why it's in the strategy
It speeds up entry into the text and doesn't waste the first seconds of the reader's attention on filler.

## When to use
- Almost always, unless the first line is itself doing interesting work.
- Especially important in short formats where every word costs something.

## How to build this type of post
1. Delete all throat-clearing before publishing.
2. Open with a thesis, conflict, benefit, or fact.
3. Check that the meaning survives after the intro is removed.
4. If an opening is necessary, it must add tension — not just announce that you have something to say.

## Examples
- Instead of "I wanted to share an important thought about pricing" — open directly with "Raising prices is easier than finding cheaper clients."
- Instead of "I caught myself thinking about this recently" — open with the thought itself.

## What to keep in mind
- The reader already knows you thought about it before writing it. You don't need to say so.
