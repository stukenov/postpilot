# Brevity

## What is this
Writing a draft freely, then cutting it in half — removing everything that doesn't carry meaning.

## Why it's in the strategy
Cutting increases idea density and makes the text easier to read in a fast-moving feed.

## When to use
- When a post drags, repeats itself, or gets lost in qualifications.
- When the idea feels stronger than the text, but the text is bloated.

## How to build this type of post
1. Write the first draft without self-censorship.
2. On the second pass, delete repetition, redundant pronouns, and over-explanation.
3. Ask about each phrase: does the meaning drop without it?
4. Keep only formulations that carry new information or maintain rhythm.

## Examples
- Instead of "I took that very hot coffee from my desk" — "Grabbed the coffee."
- Instead of "He was tired, exhausted, and drained after work" — "He was wiped out."
- Instead of "I went for a run in the park near my house for about an hour while listening to music" — "Ran for an hour this morning."

## What to keep in mind
- Brevity is not the same as dryness. Meaning, rhythm, and imagery stay — just without the padding.
