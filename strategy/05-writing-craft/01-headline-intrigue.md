# Headline: Intrigue

## What is this
The first line sparks curiosity and leaves something unsaid — enough that the reader wants to keep going.

## Why it's in the strategy
Intrigue holds attention at the entry point without revealing everything upfront, and without becoming cheap clickbait.

## When to use
- When a story, observation, or insight can be framed as a puzzle or hidden twist.
- When the goal is to create a "why?" and pull the reader into the next line.

## How to build this type of post
1. Open with an unexpected fact, a sharp question, or the promise of hidden knowledge.
2. Leave something unsaid — but don't mislead or betray the expectation.
3. Make sure the intrigue leads somewhere relevant to the reader.
4. Deliver on the promise quickly inside the post itself.

## Examples
- The habit that high-performers share — and almost never mention publicly.
- One question that immediately reveals someone's real priorities.
- The thing most founders get wrong before they even make their first hire.

## What to keep in mind
- Intrigue should ignite interest, not disguise emptiness. If the payoff is weak, the intrigue makes it worse.
