# Writing Craft

No one reads a post just because you wrote it. The reader needs to see immediately why this text is worth their time: a feeling, a useful insight, a solution, or a moment of recognition.

## Focus of this section
- Goal: Make posts readable, gripping, and reader-oriented.
- Role in the funnel: A cross-cutting quality layer — these rules apply on top of every other step in the strategy.

## How to use this section
- Treat it as an editorial checklist before publishing.
- Start with the first line and the core thesis, then cut for brevity and specificity.
- Even a strong idea loses if it's packaged in complexity, abstraction, and no rhythm.

## The 13 writing rules

1. [Headline: Intrigue](01-headline-intrigue.md) — spark curiosity without giving everything away
2. [Headline: Benefit](02-headline-benefit.md) — show the reader exactly what they'll gain
3. [Headline: Problem](03-headline-problem.md) — open with conflict or pain that demands resolution
4. [Broad Topic](04-broad-topic.md) — use everyday language instead of insider jargon
5. [One Thesis](05-one-thesis.md) — one post, one idea, nothing more
6. [Get to the Point](06-get-to-the-point.md) — cut warm-up phrasing and open with the idea itself
7. [Brevity](07-brevity.md) — write freely, then cut by half
8. [Simplicity](08-simplicity.md) — write the way you speak, not the way you present
9. [Specificity](09-specificity.md) — replace vague words with numbers, actions, and measurable results
10. [Cinematic Language](10-cinematic-language.md) — show actions and details, not named emotions
11. [Rhythm](11-rhythm.md) — vary sentence length so the text breathes
12. [Examples](12-examples.md) — anchor every principle in a real observation or case
13. [Conclusion](13-conclusion.md) — end with a new perspective, not just a summary
