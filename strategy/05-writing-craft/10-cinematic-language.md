# Cinematic Language

## What is this
Replacing descriptions of internal states with actions and details that activate the imagination and play out like a scene.

## Why it's in the strategy
It lets the reader not just understand a situation but see and feel it — which produces deeper engagement with the text.

## When to use
- When a sentence conveys a feeling or quality too abstractly.
- When the text lacks imagery and a living scene.

## How to build this type of post
1. Find places where you name an emotion or quality without showing it.
2. Translate them into actions, gestures, details, objects.
3. Choose one or two sharp details instead of a long description.
4. Make sure the imagery serves the meaning rather than decorating it.

## Examples
- Instead of "I felt very nervous before the presentation" — "I rewrote the opening slide four times and arrived 20 minutes early to pace the room alone."
- Instead of "The client was difficult" — "He replied to every email with a new revision and called it 'a small tweak.'"
- Instead of "She was a thoughtful manager" — "She remembered the name of every team member's partner and never scheduled Monday 8am calls."

## What to keep in mind
- The image should activate imagination instantly, without a long descriptive lead-in.
