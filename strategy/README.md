# Content Strategy Framework

A practical, data-driven framework for planning social media content. Built from real post metrics and engagement data, not abstract theory.

Use this as a starting template and adapt it to your niche.

## The Three-Step Funnel

Every piece of content serves one of three purposes. A healthy feed rotates through all three.

### Step 1: Instant Value

Your entry point. A first-time viewer sees this in their feed and immediately gets something useful. This is how you earn the follow.

**Formats that work:**

| Format | When to use | Example hook |
|---|---|---|
| Step-by-step guide | Audience has an urgent task | "How to audit your sales pipeline in 30 minutes" |
| Checklist | Quick self-assessment | "7 signs your leads are leaking" |
| Curated list | Audience needs recommendations | "5 tools I actually use every day" |
| Problem breakdown | Dissecting a common frustration | "Why your CRM isn't the problem" |
| Controversial take | Challenge conventional wisdom | "Networking events are a waste of time. Here's why." |
| Best practice | Share a proven technique | "The one habit that doubled my response rate" |
| Lessons from experience | First-person credibility | "3 things I'd do differently after 200 client calls" |
| How to start | Lower the barrier | "You don't need a strategy to start posting. You need 3 posts." |

**Rule:** 70–80% of your content should live here, especially when your audience is small.

### Step 2: Mindset Shift

After delivering value, shift how the audience thinks about their problem. Make them see why surface-level fixes don't work and why a deeper approach matters.

**Angles:**

- **Myth busting** — "Everyone says X. Here's what actually happens."
- **Common mistakes** — "The 3 mistakes I see in every new team."
- **Methods that don't work** — "Why adding more salespeople won't fix your pipeline."
- **Root causes** — "Your conversion problem isn't marketing. It's onboarding."
- **Warning signs** — "If your team says 'we're fine,' check these 4 things."
- **Important realizations** — "The moment I stopped chasing vanity metrics."

### Step 3: Conversion

Once trust is established, show why you specifically are the right choice. Not a hard pitch — a demonstration of competence, results, and positioning.

**Approaches:**

- **Your unique method** — what you do differently and why it works
- **Social proof** — client results, testimonials, case outcomes
- **Authority markers** — speaking, publishing, industry recognition
- **Strong positions** — what you refuse to do and why
- **Personal transformation** — your own journey and what it taught you

## Post Formats

| Format | Length | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard post** | Up to 500 chars | One thesis, one takeaway. Fast to consume. |
| **Short list** | 3–5 items | Quick tips, signs, mistakes. High save rate. |
| **Expanded list** | Detailed items | Deep-dive on a topic with context per item. |
| **Thread** | Multi-post | Complex ideas that need buildup. |
| **Series** | Recurring theme | "Day X of Y" or weekly topic rotation. |
| **Carousel** | Visual slides | Step-by-step processes, before/after. |
| **Note** | 1–3 sentences | Quick observation, hot take, question. |

## Writing Craft

### Headlines

Your first line determines whether anyone reads the rest.

| Type | Technique | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrigue** | Curiosity gap — hint at something unexpected | "The island almost nobody has heard of." |
| **Benefit** | Promise a specific result | "How to 3x your response rate in one week." |
| **Problem** | Name a pain the reader feels right now | "Your best clients are leaving. Here's the pattern." |
| **Broad topic** | Wide appeal, low barrier | "The hardest part about running a team." |

### Text Rules

1. **One thesis per post.** If you have two ideas, write two posts.
2. **Get to the point.** No warm-up paragraphs. First line = hook.
3. **Brevity wins.** Cut everything the reader doesn't need. 220–380 characters is the sweet spot for most platforms.
4. **Concrete over abstract.** "$47K lost in Q3" beats "significant revenue impact."
5. **Cinematic language.** Show the scene, don't describe the concept.
6. **Rhythm.** Mix short sentences with longer ones. Break patterns to keep attention.
7. **End with weight.** Last line should be a takeaway, a question, or a reframe — not a trailing summary.

## Operational Rules

### Content Mix

| Audience size | Value content | Personality / entertainment |
|---|---|---|
| Small (growing) | 80% | 20% |
| Medium (established) | 70% | 30% |
| Large (authority) | 60% | 40% |

Start with value. Earn the right to be entertaining.

### Posting Frequency

Consistency matters more than volume. Three strong posts per week beat ten mediocre ones.

Find a cadence you can sustain for months without burning out.

### Content Lane Rotation

Avoid posting variations of the same idea in one day. Rotate between three lanes:

1. **Novelty / proof / capability** — what's new, what works, what's possible
2. **Diagnosis / loss / business risk** — what's broken, what it costs, what's at stake
3. **Observation / story / position** — what you saw, what happened, what you believe

### Idea Sources

- Questions clients actually ask you
- Mistakes you see repeated across projects
- Industry news filtered through your experience
- Your own failures and what they taught you
- Contrarian takes you genuinely hold

## Pre-Publish Checklist

Before any post goes live, answer three questions:

1. **What is the tension in the first line?** If there's no hook, rewrite it.
2. **What is the concrete consequence?** The reader should feel urgency, not just agreement.
3. **What makes this different from the last 5 posts?** If the answer is vague, this is a duplicate angle.

If any answer is weak, rewrite the post before publishing.

## Data-Driven Editorial Rules

These rules come from analyzing real engagement data across hundreds of posts, not from theory:

- **Lead with tension.** Contrast, conflict, a question, or a concrete business pain in line 1 consistently outperforms neutral "useful" openings.
- **One claim + one consequence + one example** beats long checklists every time.
- **First-person and field observations** build more trust than abstract advice.
- **Quantify the loss.** Money, time, conversion drop, missed deals — numbers create urgency.
- **Cut list density by half.** If you have 8 items, pick the 4 strongest.
- **Stay in the 220–380 character range** unless the hook is exceptionally strong.
- **No duplicate angles in the same day.** One slot, one distinct idea.
- **Generic useful advice is not enough.** The post should create urgency, not just agreement.
