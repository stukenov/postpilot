# Strong Positions

## What is this
A clear, principled professional stance that reveals your standards, limits, and values.

## Why it's in the strategy
Separates you from generic practitioners, builds trust with the right audience, and filters out people who aren't a fit.

## When to use
- When you hold a belief that genuinely shapes your work and your product.
- When you need to stand for something, not just be likable.

## How to build this type of post
1. State the position directly — don't hedge it into softness.
2. Ground it in experience, observation, or data — not just opinion.
3. Show how this position affects how you work and what the client gets.
4. Don't dilute it into something safe just to avoid pushback.

## Examples
- Promising a specific income figure as a deliverable isn't bold marketing — it's dishonest.
- If you're starting a business to work two hours a day, you're not ready to start a business.
- Locking content behind artificial deadlines to "motivate" people is a manipulation tactic, not a pedagogy.
- If a coach guarantees a specific result in one session, that's a red flag, not a selling point.

## What to keep in mind
- A strong position works when it's backed by professional logic, not just bluntness.
- Taking a position means some people will disagree. That's the point.
