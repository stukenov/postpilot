# Controversial Takes

## What is this
A strong claim that goes against conventional wisdom and stops the reader long enough to check your logic.

## Why it's in the strategy
This format captures attention quickly and, when tied to a real problem in your niche, attracts the right audience — people who think critically about the same issues you do.

## When to use
- When you have an argued position that contradicts a widely held assumption.
- When you want to open a debate and surface your point of view.

## How to build this type of post
1. Open with the controversial claim — short, direct.
2. Acknowledge why most people believe the opposite.
3. Build your argument with examples, cause-and-effect, or observed patterns.
4. Return the reader to a practical conclusion: what should they do with this.

## Examples
- More process doesn't fix an underperforming team. Better hiring does.
- Charging less to attract more clients is the fastest way to burn out.
- Most "productivity systems" create the feeling of progress, not actual output.
- Detailed onboarding docs won't save a bad culture. They'll just document it.
- Working with fewer clients makes you a better expert, not a less successful one.

## What to keep in mind
- Provocation without argument attracts noise, not the right people.
- This format works when you're arguing toward a new conclusion, not just against an old one.
