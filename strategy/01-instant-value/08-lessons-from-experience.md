# Lessons from Experience

## What is this
A format where you take a personal experiment, a defined period of work, or a series of observations and distill them into useful takeaways.

## Why it's in the strategy
Lived experience builds trust in a way that secondhand advice cannot — you're not repeating what sounds right, you're sharing what you've actually tested and verified.

## When to use
- When you've tested something on yourself or in client work and have concrete findings.
- When your takeaways can save the reader time, money, or a preventable mistake.

## How to build this type of post
1. Briefly state what you tried and how long you ran it.
2. Collect 3–5 observations without padding.
3. Separate what worked, what didn't, and what surprised you.
4. End with the practical implication for the reader.

## Examples
- Six months of async-only work: what improved, what broke, and what I'd do differently.
- I ran discovery calls without a pitch deck for a quarter. Here's what I found.
- After auditing 30 onboarding flows, these are the three patterns that consistently fail.
- What changed after I raised my rates and stopped discounting.
- A year of daily writing: what I learned about consistency, output, and quality.

## What to keep in mind
- The experience is not valuable because it happened — it's valuable because of what you extracted from it.
- The reader should be able to identify what in your experience is applicable to theirs.
