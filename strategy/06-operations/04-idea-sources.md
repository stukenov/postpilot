# Idea Sources

## What is this
A set of reliable, repeatable sources for post topics — so you never depend on inspiration or stare at a blank content plan.

## Why it's in the strategy
It creates a steady flow of ideas from your own work, your audience, competitors, and existing content you've already proven.

## When to use
- When you feel like you've run out of things to say.
- When you need a reproducible system for finding topics, not a random one.

## How to apply
1. Track your own emotional reactions — if something triggers a strong response, write about it while it's alive.
2. Answer questions and objections from your audience in the comments.
3. If you have few comments, study the comments on competitors' posts for recurring questions.
4. Repurpose content that performed well on other platforms — adapt, don't copy.
5. Revisit your own posts that worked and write new angles on the same topic.

## Examples
- Emotional reaction: if something at work made you want to push back or explain, that's a post.
- Comments as signals: if one person asked it publicly, a hundred others had the same question and stayed silent.
- Repurposing: a talk, a newsletter issue, or a client conversation can become three posts.
- Repetition is fine: most of your audience hasn't seen your older posts, and new followers need the foundational ideas.

## What to keep in mind
- Ideas don't need to be original. They need to be yours in angle, example, and framing.
