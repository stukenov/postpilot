# Root Causes

## What is this
A post about the underlying causes that usually stay invisible while someone is busy fighting symptoms.

## Why it's in the strategy
Helps the reader reframe their situation and see that current efforts aren't working because they're aimed at the wrong layer of the problem.

## When to use
- When the standard practice in your field is to treat consequences rather than causes.
- When you need to shift attention from the surface problem to the mechanism driving it.

## How to build this type of post
1. Name a visible problem the reader already recognizes.
2. Show why the common explanations are incomplete or misleading.
3. Develop one or two real root causes with concrete examples.
4. Close with a new frame: what to notice and do differently now.

## Examples
- "You're not getting referrals because of how you close engagements, not how you start them."
- "The real reason a proposal gets ignored isn't price — it's that the problem wasn't made urgent."
- "Most content fatigue is a positioning problem, not a volume problem."
- "Clients who 'go quiet' are usually reacting to something in week one."

## What to keep in mind
- The root cause should actually change how the reader sees the situation — not just sound insightful.
- One genuinely deep cause beats five obvious observations every time.
